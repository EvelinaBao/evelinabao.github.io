## The question

On algorithmic platforms like TikTok, who actually shapes what people think and do? Influence isn't just follower counts — it's where an account sits in the network and how its rhetoric travels.

## What I did

I construct social networks of TikTok users from co-follower structures — two accounts are close when many of the same people follow both — and analyze the resulting topology to map information flow, rhetoric diffusion, and community clustering. I integrate this network structure with transformer-based language models, so the analysis captures both who reaches whom and what the messaging says.

## What I found

This project is in an early phase. Initial analyses map distinct community clusters and the pathways along which influencer messaging diffuses toward them, including patterns connecting rhetoric and algorithmic exposure to post-election polarization.

## The impact

This is the kind of analysis platform integrity and recommendation teams need: a way to see not just what content performs, but how influence structurally propagates through a user base. Early results were presented at the Political Psychology Preconference at SPSP 2026.
